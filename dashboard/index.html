Implied value = TVL × fitted multiple × token odds. The multiple is fitted from priced peers; the odds are the chance a token like this one actually trades — fitted from peers whose tokens do trade, and peers with no token at all. Protocols in between, whose ticker is recorded but whose token does not trade, are left out of the odds training, not forced into either side. Nothing here is a promise — it is a priced guess, with the misses shown below.

Token odds — can the model tell which protocols' tokens trade?

Brier score on traded-token / no-token, ten-fold and walk-forward. The base rate is the share of tracked protocols whose token trades (a positive market cap on a positive TVL). That is a narrower question than whether DefiLlama records a ticker: a recorded ticker can belong to a parent product or a line that never trades on its own, so this model answers "does the token trade?", not "is a ticker written down?". It trains on fewer protocols than a ticker-based model would — about 290 instead of 442 — and its base rate is lower, about 32% instead of 55%. What it buys for that is steadiness over time: the walk-forward Brier beats its own baseline by a wide margin, where the ticker-based target barely improves on its baseline. Family and category enter as two separate ridge groups of raw 0/1 dummies (λ=16 each), not one standardised block — the standardised block scored 0.301 walk-forward, worse than the numeric model alone. Odds carry launch uncertainty, so even a good Brier leaves wide ranges — that is why every row shows P25–P75, not a point.
